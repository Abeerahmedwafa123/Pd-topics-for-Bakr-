Observed Trends & Key Challenges

The following patterns were identified through systematic observation of 25 teachers across Foundation, Juniors, Teens, Adults, and LG Writing classes between November 2025 and May 2026 — using both a CELTA-aligned grading rubric (3.00–4.00 scale) and a structured 12-indicator performance form scored out of 48. These trends represent the most persistent and impactful issues requiring structured CPD intervention.

📵
Unprofessional Conduct During Sessions
Phone use mid-lesson observed in 5+ teachers. Directly undermines authority, models poor behaviour, and signals disengagement to students.
🔇
Weak Classroom Management Online
Cameras off, students lying in bed, Arabic use unchecked, noise unmanaged. Hamas Hamza and Alaa Ali both scored 1/4 on this indicator.
❓
ICQs Absent or Ineffective
7+ teachers give instructions without checking understanding. Students begin tasks confused, causing repeated re-explanation and lost time.
💬
Excessive Teacher Talking Time
Teachers echo students, over-explain, and complete tasks on students' behalf — significantly reducing student output time.
⚡
Low Energy & Weak Presence on Camera
Monotone delivery flagged for multiple teachers, particularly in Foundation and young learner classes where teacher energy drives engagement.
🔵
Breakout Rooms Used Without Purpose
BOR used across most classes but without structured tasks, monitoring, or post-task feedback — functioning as filler rather than learning.
🔚
Recap & Consolidation Rushed or Missing
Lesson closure is the most consistently underperforming stage. Ahmed Hussam had no recap at all; multiple others scored below Good.
🗣️
Teacher Language Inaccuracies
Pronunciation errors, wrong grammar forms, and incorrect embedded questions modelled by 5+ teachers in the CELTA observation dataset.
📋
Homework Not Clearly Administered
Several teachers skip homework or explain only one of two required tasks. Mona Salem scored 1/4; Hamas Hamza and Alaa Ali were unobserved.
Proposed CPD Session Titles & Outlines

20 sessions are proposed based on the identified observation trends. Sessions 1–4 have already been approved by Dr. Mohamed Elzayat and are marked accordingly. Sessions 5–20 are submitted for approval to complete the CPD cycle for the 2025/2026 academic year.

🔴 Critical — Address immediately
🟠 Serious — Address in first month
🟡 Important — Address in first two months
✔ Previously approved
Block 1 — Critical · Sessions 1–7
1
Stop Telling, Start Eliciting — Reducing TTT & Maximising Student Output
✔ Previously Approved
🔴 Critical
This session addresses one of the most pervasive issues across the observation cycle: teachers dominating lesson time through echoing, over-explaining, and completing tasks on students' behalf. Participants will analyse recordings of high-TTT lessons, identify unnecessary teacher intervention, and practise elicitation ladders, wait-time techniques, and student-nominating strategies. Each teacher will leave with a personalised TTT reduction plan for their next lesson.
2
Pronunciation in the Classroom — Modelling, Drilling & Correction
✔ Previously Approved
🔴 Critical
With 5+ teachers flagged for mispronouncing target vocabulary and failing to correct student errors, this session focuses on developing teachers' phonological awareness and drilling techniques. Topics include phoneme isolation, word stress, connected speech, choral and individual drilling, and how to correct pronunciation without interrupting fluency. Teachers will also practise the MPF framework (Meaning, Form, Pronunciation) for presenting vocabulary accurately.
3
Lesson Closure & Language Accuracy — Recap, Grammar Clarification & Embedded Questions
✔ Previously Approved
🔴 Critical
Recap stages are consistently rushed or missing, and several teachers produce inaccurate grammar models. This session covers: how to design a purposeful 5–10 minute lesson closure, student-led recap strategies, exit ticket techniques, and a focused grammar accuracy workshop on structures most commonly misused — including reported speech, embedded questions, and conditional forms.
4
Living the Immerse — Bringing the Monglish Philosophy to Life in Your Classroom
✔ Previously Approved
🔴 Critical
This session reconnects teachers with the Monglish institutional philosophy and its practical classroom implications. It addresses Arabic use, camera and background policy enforcement, the spirit behind the Dojo points system, and what 'immersive English' genuinely looks like online. Teachers leave with concrete strategies for upholding the Monglish approach consistently rather than treating policies as optional.
5
Professionalism in the Online Classroom
🔴 Critical
Mobile phone use during sessions was observed in over five teachers. This session covers the full scope of professional conduct in an online environment: phone use, consistent camera presence, punctuality, appropriate dress code, and what 'being present' means when teaching remotely. Scenarios reflect on how unprofessional behaviours impact student trust, engagement, and institutional reputation. Teachers will co-create a Professional Conduct Agreement for their classrooms.
6
Classroom Management Strategies for the Online Environment
🔴 Critical
Managing a virtual classroom requires a different toolkit to in-person teaching. This session targets managing cameras-off students, noise and background disruptions, Arabic use during English tasks, students joining late or in inappropriate settings, and turn-taking in whole-class discussions. Teachers role-play challenging scenarios and build a classroom management toolkit tailored to their specific class level and type.
7
ICQs That Actually Work — Checking Understanding Before Tasks Begin
🔴 Critical
ICQs are absent or poorly executed in 7+ observed teachers. This workshop distinguishes effective from ineffective ICQs, showing why questions like "Do you understand?" are useless. Teachers will write and deliver ICQs for real Monglish tasks from their own materials, practise staging complex multi-step instructions, and peer-review each other's ICQ design. A reference card of ICQ templates will be produced for classroom use.
Block 2 — Serious · Sessions 8–13
8
Designing Purposeful Breakout Rooms
🟠 Serious
This session moves beyond 'send them to breakout' — covering task design with clear outcomes, how to monitor multiple rooms effectively, timing strategies, and how to conduct meaningful whole-class feedback after BOR activities. Each teacher will redesign an existing BOR activity from their upcoming lessons using the frameworks introduced, and trial it before the next session.
9
Energy, Presence & Voice — Teaching with Impact in an Online Classroom
🟠 Serious
Low energy and flat delivery were flagged for multiple teachers, particularly in Foundation and young learner classes. This session is practical and experiential: teachers work on voice modulation, pacing and pausing, facial expressions on camera, physical energy management across a 90-minute session, and sustaining enthusiasm in repetitive or revision-heavy lessons. Video examples and peer feedback will be used throughout.
10
Giving Feedback That Teaches — Error Correction Techniques
🟠 Serious
Observation data shows feedback is either absent, purely positive, or teacher-dominated. This session introduces a range of error correction strategies: recasting, prompting, metalinguistic clues, and explicit correction. Teachers practise identifying the right moment to correct, how to involve peers in correction, and how to use errors as teaching opportunities. The session also covers written feedback on student work in LG Writing classes.
11
Writing Clear Instructions — The Foundation of Every Successful Task
🟠 Serious
Many task failures traced in the observation data originate not in the task itself but in unclear or cognitively overloaded instructions. This workshop focuses on simplifying language for level, staging multi-step tasks, using models and demonstrations, and knowing when written instructions support or replace verbal ones. Teachers rewrite real observed instruction sequences and test them with peers.
12
Lesson Staging & Timing — Pacing a Lesson That Works
🟠 Serious
Across both observation datasets, production and recap stages are consistently cut short because earlier stages overrun. This session covers realistic time allocation, managing overrunning tasks without losing the lesson arc, protecting freer practice and consolidation stages, and making meaningful micro-adjustments mid-lesson. Teachers will time-map their upcoming lesson plans and discuss potential pinch-points in pairs.
13
CCQs — Checking Concept Understanding, Not Just Instructions
🟠 Serious
While ICQs check that students understand what to do, Concept Checking Questions verify they understand the meaning behind language. Multiple teachers use CCQs poorly or not at all, leading to students practising language they have not understood. This session defines the ICQ/CCQ distinction, provides frameworks for designing CCQs for grammar and vocabulary, and gives teachers extended practice with real Monglish lesson content.
Block 3 — Important · Sessions 14–20
14
MPF in Practice — Teaching Vocabulary the Right Way
🟡 Important
Several teachers present vocabulary incidentally without addressing Meaning, Form, and Pronunciation systematically. This results in students practising words they cannot spell, stress correctly, or use in the right grammatical pattern. This session works through the full MPF framework using real Monglish vocabulary sets, shows teachers how to sequence a vocabulary presentation stage efficiently, and addresses common teacher errors in vocabulary instruction.
15
Homework Administration — Making the Most of the Final 5 Minutes
🟡 Important
Homework is either skipped, mentioned without explanation, or only one of the two required tasks (writing and speaking) is addressed. This session covers how to introduce and explain both tasks clearly within the final five minutes, how to check student understanding of homework expectations using ICQs, and how to link homework meaningfully to lesson content so students understand its purpose.
16
Student Inclusion & Differentiation in Mixed-Ability Online Classes
🟡 Important
Several classes contain learners at significantly different levels, with stronger students dominating and weaker ones going unsupported. This session covers differentiation strategies for an online context: scaffolding tasks by level, strategic nomination, extension activities for fast finishers, supporting struggling students within a whole-class structure, and identifying when a student needs to be flagged for academic support outside the classroom.
17
Making Activities Count — From Fun to Learning
🟡 Important
Games and interactive activities are widely used but frequently left unexploited: the activity ends, points are tallied, and learning stops there. This session teaches teachers how to design feedback loops into every activity, use game results diagnostically, draw language out of game contexts, and debrief activities in a way that consolidates rather than just concludes. Teachers will analyse existing Monglish games and redesign their follow-up stages.
18
Setting & Enforcing Classroom Rules Consistently
🟡 Important
Background compliance, camera policy, Arabic use, and student posture are near-universally enforced inconsistently. This session focuses on how to introduce classroom rules clearly at the start of a course, reinforce them session by session without confrontation, handle non-compliance professionally, and involve students in co-creating expectations so they feel ownership rather than imposition. Role-play scenarios will be used throughout.
19
Understanding & Using the Observation Rubric — Knowing What You Are Being Assessed On
🟡 Important
Many teachers receive observation feedback without a clear understanding of the 12-indicator rubric used to assess them or how scores are derived. This session demystifies the entire assessment framework: what each indicator means, what 'Exemplary' versus 'Good' versus 'Satisfactory' looks like in practice, and how teachers can use the rubric proactively as a self-assessment tool between observations. Teachers will score sample lesson recordings, discuss discrepancies, and leave with a personal development checklist mapped to their own most recent observation report.
20
Building a Positive Online Classroom Culture from Session One
🟡 Important
The challenges observed mid-course — disengaged students, cameras off, Arabic use, poor posture, late arrivals — are often rooted in how the first session was conducted. This session focuses on course setup: how to introduce yourself and establish authority warmly, how to co-create classroom agreements with students from day one, how to set camera and background expectations clearly and positively, and how to build routines in the first three sessions that make classroom management easier for the rest of the course. Role-play and planning templates will be used throughout.
Closing Note
The 20 sessions proposed in this document represent a complete CPD response to the patterns identified in the 2025/2026 observation cycle. Sessions 1–4, previously approved by Dr. Mohamed Elzayat, are already scheduled and will serve as the foundation of the programme. Sessions 5–20 are designed to complement and extend that foundation, addressing the full range of observed needs across Foundation, Juniors, Teens, Adults, and LG Writing teachers.

It is recommended that sessions be delivered in priority order — grouping 🔴 Critical sessions in Block 1 (Weeks 1–4), 🟠 Serious sessions in Block 2 (Weeks 5–9), and 🟡 Important sessions in Block 3 (Weeks 10–14) — allowing for reflection and re-observation between blocks to measure impact.
